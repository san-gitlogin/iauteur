# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be kind. Assume the other person is trying. Critique the work, not the person.

That's the whole thing, and for a project this size it's usually enough. The rest of this page is
what happens when it isn't.

## What's expected

- **Assume good faith.** A confused question, a wrong patch, or a bug report missing half the details
  is someone trying to help with incomplete information. Fill in the gap rather than scoring a point.
- **Criticise the code, not the coder.** "This breaks in vertical" is useful. "Did you even look at
  it?" is not, even when it's tempting and even when it's true.
- **"I don't know" is welcome** — from contributors and from the maintainer.
- **Beginners are not a burden.** Everyone rendering their first video is confused by the same three
  things. If you were confused, that's a documentation bug worth reporting.
- **Respect the review.** A PR can be declined, or asked for changes, and that isn't a verdict on
  you. Equally, a maintainer explaining *why* is part of the job.

## What isn't acceptable

Harassment, insults, or personal attacks. Discriminatory or demeaning comments about anyone's
identity — including but not limited to age, disability, ethnicity, gender identity or expression,
nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, caste, or sexual orientation. Sexualised language
or advances. Publishing someone's private information. Deliberate intimidation, stalking, or
sustained disruption.

None of that is welcome in issues, pull requests, commit messages, code, or anywhere else this
project's name is on.

## Scope

Applies in all project spaces, and when you're representing the project in public.

## Reporting

If someone's behaviour is a problem, report it to the maintainer **privately** — do not fight it out
in a public thread.

Use GitHub's [private vulnerability reporting form](https://github.com/san-gitlogin/iauteur/security/advisories/new)
if you have nothing else; it is a private channel to the maintainer and works for conduct reports as
well as security ones. If you'd rather use another route, open an issue asking for a private contact
without naming anyone or describing the incident.

Reports will be read and kept confidential. Please be honest about what happened, and be patient:
this is a solo, spare-time project with no moderation team behind it.

## Enforcement

The maintainer may warn, ask for an apology, edit or delete comments, decline contributions, or block
an account, depending on severity. Repeated or serious violations mean a permanent ban.

Because there is exactly one maintainer, there is no appeals committee — a fact worth stating plainly
rather than pretending otherwise. If the problem is *with* the maintainer, forking is always
available to you: the project is MIT licensed precisely so nobody needs permission to carry on
elsewhere.
